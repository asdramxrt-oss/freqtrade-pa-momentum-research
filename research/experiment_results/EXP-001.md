## 1. Purpose

Establish a clean, boring, fully specified reference trend follower with **no ML,
no regime classifier, no router and no parameter tuning**, and measure honestly
whether the classic Turtle premise survives realistic crypto costs and
out-of-sample data.

Implementation: `user_data/strategies/turtle/DonchianTurtleBaseline.py`
Signal logic: `user_data/strategies/shared/pa_signals.py`
Indicators: `user_data/strategies/shared/pa_indicators.py`
Risk: `user_data/strategies/shared/pa_risk.py`

## 9. Limitations and unresolved questions

1. **Compounded sizing inflates the headline.** Wallet-relative sizing means
   late-sample trades are far larger than early-sample trades, so +4817% is
   hard to compare with anything. Per-trade statistics (profit factor,
   expectancy, p-value) are the comparable numbers.
2. **Per-trade significance is not established.** p = 0.066 means the mean trade
   is not distinguishable from zero at 5%. A profitable equity curve driven by a
   handful of large winners is consistent with luck.
3. **Slippage is not separately modelled.** Freqtrade has no native slippage
   model, so costs were swept via the fee parameter only. Real fill quality on
   breakout entries (which are by construction executed into momentum) is
   probably worse than a flat fee.
4. **No short side.** Long-only spot cannot express downtrends; 2022 and 2025
   losses are structural, not a bug.
5. **Effective sample size.** 1,347 trades across 10 highly-correlated crypto
   assets in 7 years is far fewer independent observations than the trade count
   suggests, because crypto pairs co-move.
6. **Pair dispersion is extreme** (BNB +1503% vs LINK −642%), which is the
   single-pair-dependence failure mode the charter warns about.
7. **One OOS evaluation performed.** Recorded here for transparency; no
   additional OOS runs may be made for this version without a new experiment ID.

## 10. Decision

**FAIL.** The raw Turtle baseline is not evidence of a deployable edge:
per-trade expectancy is not statistically significant, the untouched OOS year is
negative, and the drawdown is far beyond any acceptable production level.

It is **retained as the reference baseline** (charter §5) because its purpose is
comparison, not deployment.

Consequences for the programme:

1. Do **not** "fix" the baseline by adding filters or tuning periods. That would
   destroy its value as a reference.
2. Proceed to **EXP-002** — Turtle with realistic transaction costs — to complete
   the cost analysis (0.10% level, per-year cost runs, explicit fee and turnover
   totals, and slippage-assumption sensitivity).
3. The ML programme (EXP-010) has a concrete, measurable target: raise
   per-trade expectancy significance while reducing drawdown, measured against
   these exact numbers.

Recorded in `research/decisions/DECISION_LOG.md` as **DEC-002**.
