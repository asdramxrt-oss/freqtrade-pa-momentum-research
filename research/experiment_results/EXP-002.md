**Turnover** is the sum of entry and exit notional. Freqtrade's printed
"Total trade volume" is `turnover + fees` (its costs include the fee), so the
reported figure is slightly larger than the notional turnover above; this is
recorded rather than silently reconciled.

**Reading the fee-drag column.** Fees consume 14.6% of gross profit at the
optimistic base fee and 49.0% at the stress fee. Beyond ~0.80% they consume the
entire gross profit.

Turnover *falls* as the fee rises (164.5M → 11.7M notional) because compounded
wallet sizing shrinks each position as the fee drag reduces equity. Fee **paid**
in USDT is therefore not monotonic; the ratio fee/gross-profit is the stable
measure of cost drag.

## 5. Results — cost sensitivity (the core finding)

Profit factor and net return fall monotonically with the fee; drawdown rises
monotonically:

| Fee/side | PF | Net return | Wallet max DD |
|----------|----|------------|---------------|
| 0.05% | 1.228 | +4,817.29% | 61.55% |
| 0.10% | 1.197 | +3,638.73% | 65.07% |
| 0.20% | 1.145 | +2,067.47% | 71.18% |
| 0.30% | 1.103 | +1,151.36% | 76.23% |
| 0.50% | 1.044 | +306.54% | 85.42% |
| 1.00% | 0.972 | −72.82% | 97.69% |

Breakeven points, linearly interpolated on the full sample:

| Threshold | Fee/side | Round-trip cost |
|-----------|----------|-----------------|
| Profit factor = 1.00 | 0.804% | 1.61% |
| Net return = 0 | 0.904% | 1.81% |

**Interpretation.** The edge is genuinely cost-sensitive (a 4× fee increase from
0.05% to 0.20% removes ~57% of the compounded return and pushes drawdown from
61.6% to 71.2%), but it does **not** fail because of cost. It survives every
pre-registered cost threshold — A2 (PF ≥ 1.10 at base), A8 (PF ≥ 1.05 at stress)
and M1 (PF ≥ 1.10 at mid) all pass — and it only stops working at roughly 0.80%
per side, which is about 4× the stress assumption and far above realistic
taker-plus-slippage for these pairs.

## 9. Limitations and slippage-assumption sensitivity

1. **Slippage is modelled as a flat per-side cost.** Freqtrade has no native
   slippage model, so the fee is the only cost channel. Read as a slippage
   sweep: 0.05% = taker only; 0.10% = taker + ~0.05% slippage; 0.20% = taker +
   ~0.15% slippage. Because the PF = 1.00 breakeven is 0.804% per side (1.61%
   round trip), a slippage assumption would have to exceed roughly 4× the stress
   scenario before it changed the cost verdict. The result is therefore **not
   fragile to the slippage assumption within any plausible range** — which makes
   the A3/A4/A5/A6 failures more, not less, important.
2. **Breakout entries may slip asymmetrically.** The sweep charges the same cost
   on entry and exit; real breakout fills (by construction into momentum) are
   likely worse on entry. A symmetric flat fee is an optimistic model of the
   shape of the cost, even if the level is bracketed.
3. **Fees paid in USDT is not monotonic** because compounded wallet sizing shrinks
   positions as the fee rises. Fee/gross-profit is the interpretable cost measure;
   the absolute fee totals are reported for completeness.
4. **Compounded sizing still inflates the headline.** Per-trade statistics
   (profit factor, p-value) remain the comparable numbers, as in EXP-001.
5. **Effective sample size** is unchanged: 1,347 trades across 10 co-moving
   crypto assets are far fewer independent observations than the count suggests.
6. **One OOS evaluation is selection-relevant** for this strategy version
   (EXP-001); the three runs here are cost views and were not used to select
   anything.
7. **Turnover shrinks with the fee**, so the cost analysis measures the strategy
   *as it responds to cost* (it trades less when costs rise), not a fixed-trade
   counterfactual.

## 10. Decision

**FAIL.** The raw Turtle baseline still fails as standalone edge evidence: the
same four criteria fail at every fee level. EXP-002's contribution is the
cost characterisation, and its conclusion is deliberately narrow and negative:

- Costs are a real but **not binding** constraint. Fees consume 14.6% of gross
  profit at base and 49.0% at stress, but the strategy survives all
  pre-registered cost thresholds and only stops working at ~0.80% per side.
- The failures are **not** cost-driven: per-trade expectancy is not significant
  (p = 0.066), the untouched 2025 holdout is negative (PF 0.80), and wallet
  drawdown is 61.55%. Raising the fee worsens all three; lowering it does not
  fix any of them.

Consequences for the programme:

1. The baseline stays frozen and is **not** to be tuned, including for cost
   (charter §5, DEC-002).
2. No parameter was optimised and no OOS number was used to select anything.
3. EXP-003 (Pullback continuation standalone) is **not** started automatically.
   The frozen sequence requires an explicit decision before the next experiment;
   EXP-002's decision is recorded here as **DEC-003** and the programme stops
   pending that decision.
4. Any future strategy must beat EXP-001's per-trade statistics *and* remain
   above the cost thresholds demonstrated here.

Recorded in `research/decisions/DECISION_LOG.md` as **DEC-003**.
